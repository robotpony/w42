<!-- #todo Thesis, in your words: I suspect the most difficult thing in software development has become containing scope creep and curating high quality (useful, interesting, simple) applications. As development time approaches zero, scope creep approaches infinity. -->

<!-- #todo The law: "Scope expands to fill the capacity to build it. Quality holds only as far as you can verify it." Parkinson-shaped; Jevons paradox is the reason (cheaper code, more code). Zawinski's law is the old joke version. Brooks's second-system effect and No Silver Bullet are optional support. -->

<!-- #todo The mechanism: more scope means more testing, which means longer to verify quality, and at some point it's impossible to complete or balance. Verification grows faster than features, because features interact. Building got cheap; judging didn't. -->

<!-- #todo The balance: software has always balanced viable ideas, salable ideas, and viable approaches (can it be solved at all). Two sides of it here: too little thought and it falls over (slop), too much scope and it falls over the same way (verification debt). Eyes bigger than your stomach. -->
